---
title: A bumblebee in a cold spring
date: 2025-09-19
season: spring
speciesId: bombus-terrestris
---

The spring palette went up on the plates this week and the bumblebee is the
drawing it flatters least. Every other specimen in the collection is a hard
animal — cases, plates, a shell of some kind — and the ochres and olives of a
southern September sit on them like ink on paper. A bee is fur, and fur is the
one thing three line weights cannot do.

What we draw instead is the arrangement: the collar, the band across the
second segment, the buff tail that gives
[_Bombus terrestris_](/specimen/bombus-terrestris) its name, and the four wings
held out where Morgan drew them in 1896. The hairs are implied by hatching that
follows the thorax rather than crossing it. Stand at arm’s length from the
screen and it reads as pile; stand closer and it is plainly a set of lines, and
we would rather that than a soft edge pretending to be a photograph.

A note for whoever redraws her. The reference is a lithograph from Saunders,
not a specimen, and it was chosen for exactly that reason: a pinned bumblebee’s
fur mats within a season of being pinned, and matted fur cannot be un-drawn. We
would sooner inherit an engraver’s abstraction than a hundred-year-old accident
of storage.
