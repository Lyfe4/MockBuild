Every drawing in this collection is opaque except one. The southern hawker has
four wings, they overlap each other and the abdomen, and what is behind them is
the whole point of the animal — so the hawker’s wings are the only ink here that
is genuinely see-through.

It took some arguing. A wash of the surface colour would have been simpler and
would have been a lie: a wing laid over an abdomen would have hidden the
abdomen, and the reader would have been told that a dragonfly is four flat
paddles rather than four sheets of glass. The concession is narrow and it is
written into the rules — only a forewing or a hindwing may ask for it, and a
check refuses the request anywhere else. A translucent thorax would be a mistake
nobody could see.

[_Aeshna cyanea_](/specimen/aeshna-cyanea) cost us in another way too. Lucas
draws each figure on his plate with the wings spread to one side and the legs
splayed to the other, so that two animals fit a page without overlapping. Ours
has both on both sides. The wings were measured off one side of his figure, the
legs off the other, and each was folded back across the midline.

Nine strokes to a wing. Lucas drew several hundred cells.
